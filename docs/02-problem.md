# Problem Discovery

## Problem Statement

People living with chronic injuries often struggle to organize years of medical information across multiple healthcare providers. Their medical history becomes fragmented, making it difficult to understand their progress, communicate effectively with new doctors, and make informed healthcare decisions.

---

## Background

Managing a chronic injury is rarely a single medical appointment. Patients often spend months or years visiting different healthcare professionals while trying various treatments and undergoing multiple diagnostic tests.

Throughout this journey they accumulate information from many different sources, including:

- MRI and X-ray reports
- Blood test results
- Doctor visit notes
- Physiotherapy sessions
- Rehabilitation exercises
- Medications
- Injections and procedures
- Personal observations and symptom logs

Because this information is scattered across different systems, documents, and personal notes, patients often struggle to maintain a complete picture of their healthcare journey.

---

## Pain Points

People managing chronic injuries commonly experience the following challenges:

### Repeating Their Medical History

Patients frequently need to explain the same history every time they visit a new healthcare professional.

### Fragmented Information

Medical records, imaging reports, prescriptions, personal notes, and treatment histories are often stored in different locations with no unified timeline.

### Difficulty Tracking Progress

Over time it becomes difficult to remember:

- when symptoms started
- which treatments were attempted
- what provided temporary relief
- what did not improve the condition

### Poor Preparation for Medical Appointments

Without a structured history, patients may forget important details during appointments, making communication with healthcare professionals less effective.

### Time, Financial, and Emotional Cost

Repeated consultations, unsuccessful treatments, and the effort required to organize medical information can lead to frustration and unnecessary stress.

---

## Existing Solutions

People currently rely on a variety of tools to manage their healthcare information, but each has significant limitations.

### Notes Applications

Useful for quick note-taking but lack structure, relationships between information, and chronological organization.

### Spreadsheets

Flexible but difficult to maintain over long periods and not designed for healthcare tracking.

### Hospital and Clinic Portals

Provide official medical records but are usually limited to a single healthcare provider and rarely include personal observations or information from other clinics.

### Memory

Many patients rely on memory during appointments, which becomes increasingly difficult as the number of visits, treatments, and tests grows.

---

## Opportunity

There is an opportunity to build a personal healthcare journal that focuses on organization rather than diagnosis.

By providing a structured timeline of injuries, symptoms, treatments, medical visits, and diagnostic tests, patients can better understand their healthcare journey and communicate more effectively with healthcare professionals.

The application complements existing healthcare systems by helping patients organize their own information rather than replacing medical records or clinical decision-making.

---

## Why This Matters

Living with a chronic injury often involves a long and complex healthcare journey. Patients invest significant time, money, and emotional energy seeking answers while navigating multiple healthcare providers and treatment options.

A well-organized personal health journal can reduce frustration by helping users maintain a complete, chronological record of their medical history. Better organization supports clearer communication with healthcare professionals and allows patients to participate more confidently in discussions about their care.

The goal is not to provide medical advice or diagnoses, but to help patients better understand and communicate their own healthcare journey.
